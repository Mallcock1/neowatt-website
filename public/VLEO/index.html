Section 1 · The constraint

Thruster power scales steeply with altitude.

Drag rises sharply as you descend into VLEO, and thruster power follows – from ~300 W at 250 km to ~4 kW at 150 km. An order of magnitude in 100 km.

For a fixed power budget, that curve is a hard altitude floor.

Section 2 · The power-limited floor

Your budget sets the lowest altitude you can hold.

A 1.6 kW bus with 0.8 kW for payload leaves 0.8 kW for thrusters. Where that line meets the demand curve – around 205 km – is your practical limit.

Going lower means more solar or more battery. Both add drag and mass – fighting the goal.

Section 3 · Going lower

Beamed power lowers the altitude floor.

NEOWATT beams laser power from a transmitter satellite in sunlight to the receiver. Even 0.3 kW delivered to the bus shifts the floor.

At 0.5 kW, expect 15–20 km lower without resizing. At 1 kW, sub-180 km becomes feasible.

Section 4 · Through eclipse

Longer payload duty cycle in the umbra.

In eclipse, payload draw comes from batteries. High-power instruments – IR, radar, active comms – drain capacity fast. Beamed power from a transmitter still in sunlight cuts the net battery drain.

VLEO eclipses last 30–35 min. A partial supplement is the difference between a curtailed and a complete pass.

Section 5 · Breaking the death spiral

A larger bus, without the feedback loop.

Bigger bus → more drag → more power required → bigger solar array → more drag → more power required → …

Beamed power lands on the bus without adding drag (or via a small dome – a fraction of an array's footprint). It's the only input that breaks the loop.

The result: more sustainable payload at every altitude, and feasibility where the loop otherwise rules you out.

Section 6 · Receiver options

Generic panel, or specialised dome?

Generic panel – beam onto your existing array. No new hardware, no extra drag. ~25% efficiency, narrow acceptance (~±30°).

Specialised dome – optimised for the laser wavelength. ~60% efficiency, wider acceptance (~±75°). Adds a small aperture to the slipstream.

The drag question depends on geometry: the smaller solar array enabled by beamed power often more than offsets the dome.

Model assumptions
  • Thruster power vs altitude: exponential fit through (250 km, 300 W), (200 km, 1 kW), (150 km, 4 kW), consistent with VLEO drag–power scaling reported in Crisp et al. (2020), The benefits of very low earth orbit for earth observation missions.
  • Atmosphere: log-linear density between 200 km (2.5×10⁻¹⁰ kg/m³) and 250 km (7×10⁻¹¹ kg/m³), per NRLMSISE-00 nominal solar conditions.
  • Orbital velocity: 7.8 km/s, treated as constant. v varies only ~1.5% (7.82 → 7.70 km/s) over 150–350 km, dwarfed by the ~10× density variation that dominates drag.
  • Solar flux: 1366 W/m² (Kopp & Lean 2011). PV efficiency: 30%, representative of triple-junction GaAs cells (Spectrolab XTJ Prime).
  • Drag coefficient: Cd = 2.2, free-molecular flat plate (Cook 1965; Moe & Moe 2005).
  • Generic-panel laser efficiency: 25%, ±30° acceptance, typical of Si/III–V single-wavelength response.
  • Specialised dome: 60% efficiency, ±75° acceptance, 0.3 m² added cross-section, consistent with reported wavelength-tuned PV demonstrations (Steinsiek et al.; LaserMotive).
  • Eclipse runtime: while in shadow, the payload draws on battery alone. Beamed power reduces the net drain rate to (payload – delivered), so the same battery lasts longer.
  • Sustainable payload: at a given altitude, the maximum payload power is what's left after the array generates electricity and the thrusters spend it on drag. With a beam, the equation gains a term that costs no drag area:
    payloadmax = (array area × solar flux × PV η) + delivered beam – drag-power-per-m² × (array area + bus area)
    The drag-power-per-m² constant is fitted so the model reproduces the 200 km thruster-power data point.
  • Drag delta: "today" means the current array area with no dome. When beaming supplies a share of the bus power, the array can shrink in proportion (less drag). A specialised dome adds a small drag penalty back. The net change can be positive or negative depending on which dominates.
